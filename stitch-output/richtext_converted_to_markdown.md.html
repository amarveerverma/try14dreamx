Document purpose and authority ================================== 1.1 Purpose ----------- This document defines how the DreamX product should be presented, navigated, experienced and implemented on the frontend. It is the frontend implementation companion to DreamX\_PRD\_v2.0.md. It covers: * Product information architecture. * Student, mentor and admin experiences. * Page and route structure. * Screen-level requirements. * Navigation and responsive layouts. * Design system and reusable components. * User interactions and state handling. * Forms, validation and feedback. * Loading, empty, success and error states. * Frontend architecture. * Backend integration boundaries. * Accessibility. * Frontend security. * Testing and acceptance criteria. This document is not a replacement for the PRD. If a frontend recommendation conflicts with a confirmed PRD business rule, the PRD takes precedence. 1.2 Requirement labels ---------------------- Use these labels throughout implementation: LabelMeaning**CONFIRMED**Established product or business decision**MVP**Required for the initial marketplace release**PHASE 2**Deferred AI tools, AI Credits and cashback functionality**PROPOSED UX**Recommended frontend design or interaction, pending product review**OPEN**Requires a business or product decision**BLOCKED**Must not be implemented as an assumed capability 1.3 Frontend objectives ----------------------- DreamX must not look or behave like a generic mentor-listing website. The experience should communicate four things: 1. **Direction:** Help students understand what to do next. 2. **Trust:** Make mentor verification and service expectations understandable. 3. **Progress:** Show evidence-backed career improvement over time. 4. **Accountability:** Make bookings, reports, payments and service outcomes transparent. The frontend should make the product feel like a guided career improvement platform with a human-service marketplace at its centre. 2\. Product experience principles ================================= 2.1 Core principles ------------------- ### Principle 1 — Goal-first, not catalogue-first Students should be able to start with a problem or career goal rather than needing to understand ten service names. Example: > I have an upcoming technical interview and need to improve my problem-solving and interview performance. DreamX should translate that goal into relevant services and explain why they are recommended. ### Principle 2 — Show the next useful action Every important dashboard should answer: * Where am I currently? * What has happened? * What needs my attention? * What should I do next? Avoid dashboards filled with unrelated cards, charts and decorative statistics. ### Principle 3 — Explain recommendations A recommendation should show its reason. For example: * Technical Mock Interview — recommended because interview skills have not yet been assessed. * Resume Review — recommended because the current resume assessment identified improvement areas. Do not display unexplained recommendation percentages or fabricated AI reasoning. ### Principle 4 — Trust before conversion Students should be able to understand: * Who the mentor is. * What has been verified. * Which services the mentor is approved to deliver. * What the service includes. * What they will receive after the session. * What happens if the booking cannot be fulfilled. ### Principle 5 — Progress must be evidence-based A readiness score should be accompanied by: * Assessment date. * Assessed dimensions. * Evidence or feedback. * Unassessed dimensions. * Suggested actions. Do not use arbitrary progress animations or automatically increase scores because a student has completed a booking. ### Principle 6 — Never hide commercial details Before a booking or subscription, clearly display the price, applicable discount, final payable amount and relevant terms. ### Principle 7 — Avoid unnecessary friction Ask only for information needed at the current stage. Do not make optional information mandatory just to complete onboarding or view the marketplace. 3\. Service booking experience =============================== 11.1 Booking entry points ------------------------- A student can begin a booking from: * Service detail page. * Mentor profile. * Recommendation card. * Dashboard next action. All entry points should lead to a consistent booking flow. 11.2 Booking flow ----------------- Proposed sequence: 1. Select service. 2. Choose booking mode. 3. Select mentor or Smart Match. 4. Select time or preferred windows. 5. Review booking details. 6. Review price and policies. 7. Continue to payment. 8. Verify payment and booking status. 9. Show confirmation or recovery state. 11.3 Direct booking ------------------- **Purpose:** Student selects a specific approved mentor and available time slot. ### Screen elements * Mentor summary. * Service. * Calendar. * Available time slots. * Timezone. * Session duration. * Price summary. * Continue action. ### Calendar behavior * Disable unavailable slots. * Show loading while availability is fetched. * Refresh or revalidate before checkout. * Display local time clearly. * Do not assume a slot remains available because it appeared in an earlier response. The backend must own slot locking and final booking confirmation. ### Slot-lock states * Available. * Temporarily held. * Unavailable. * Expired. * Confirmed. The frontend must reflect the server response. 11.4 Smart Match ---------------- **Purpose:** Student requests an eligible mentor through dispatch rather than selecting a particular mentor. ### Screen elements * Service summary. * Explanation of Smart Match. * Preferred time-window selection. * Price summary. * Expected process. * Submit request action. ### Time-window selection The student must be able to understand and edit their preferred availability windows. Validate: * Date and time. * Timezone. * Past times. * Overlapping or duplicate windows. * Required number of valid windows according to backend policy. The old v1 proposed three-window requirement should not be hardcoded unless confirmed in the final business rules. ### Dispatch status screen Show: * Request submitted. * Matching in progress. * Current dispatch stage. * Mentor accepted, if any. * Timeout and fallback information. The established timing direction: * Wave 2 begins after 30 minutes if no mentor has accepted. * At two hours without acceptance, offer an alternative open slot or full refund. ### Critical interaction The student must explicitly select the alternative slot or refund option. Never automatically reschedule the student. ### State model * Draft. * Submitting. * Payment pending. * Matching. * Wave 2. * Mentor accepted. * Confirmed. * Alternative slot offered. * Awaiting student decision. * Refund requested. * Refunded. * Failed. The actual state must come from the backend. 11.5 Booking checkout --------------------- Before payment, display: * Service. * Mentor, if selected. * Date and time. * Duration. * Listed price. * Membership discount. * Bundle discount, if applicable. * Final payable amount. * Cancellation policy. * What happens if no mentor accepts. * Payment provider information. ### Discount presentation Membership and custom bundle discounts do not stack. Apply the better eligible discount. Do not calculate financial values independently in the browser as authoritative values. The server must return the payable amount and its breakdown. ### Payment states * Not started. * Processing. * Successful. * Failed. * Pending verification. * Booking confirmed. * Booking not confirmed. A successful redirect is not proof of payment. Wait for authoritative server verification. 12\. Frontend state and interaction standards ============================================= 24.1 Server-authoritative state ------------------------------- The backend owns: * Authentication and role. * Mentor verification. * Service approval. * Slot availability. * Booking state. * Dispatch state. * Payment status. * Refund status. * Report delivery status. * Readiness score. * Membership status. * AI Credit balance. The frontend can optimistically update non-critical visual state, but must reconcile it with the server. 24.2 State categories --------------------- Separate: 1. Server data. 2. Local component state. 3. Form state. 4. URL/query state. 5. Authentication/session state. Avoid duplicating the same server record across multiple unrelated client stores. 24.3 Form behavior ------------------ Every form should include: * Labels. * Help text where needed. * Validation. * Submission state. * Success feedback. * Recoverable error feedback. * Prevention of accidental duplicate submission. Validation should exist on both client and server. 24.4 Loading states ------------------- Use: * Skeletons for predictable page structures. * Inline loading for small actions. * Progress indicators for longer operations. * Persistent status panels for payment or dispatch workflows. Avoid blank screens while data loads. 24.5 Empty states ----------------- Every list-based page needs an intentional empty state. Examples: * No bookings yet. * No reports delivered. * No mentor offers. * No notifications. * No available slots. * No search results. Each empty state should explain what it means and offer a relevant next action. 24.6 Error states ----------------- Errors should be: * Human-readable. * Actionable where possible. * Free of internal stack traces. * Safe to retry where appropriate. * Logged through the appropriate monitoring system. 24.7 Confirmation dialogs ------------------------- Use confirmation for: * Cancellation. * Refund request. * Mentor acceptance where necessary. * Verification decisions. * Destructive account actions. * Sensitive admin changes. Do not put a confirmation dialog in front of every ordinary action. 25\.
